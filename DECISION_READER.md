# Interpreting the frozen iteration-2000 player

The requested outcome is readable language that accurately reflects the player's
decisions. The player always uses its original action path. Reader outputs cannot
change its activations, weights, or selected move.

## What constitutes evidence

Board-fact accuracy is a prerequisite, not decision fidelity. We distinguish:

1. **Decision reporting:** Can generated language identify the deployed move's
   source, direction, and full/half troop amount? Does the surrounding description
   state correct, observable destination facts?
2. **Behavioral interpretation:** Does explanatory content help an independently
   trained observer reconstruct the policy beyond an observation-only baseline?
   Removing the explicit action sentence is necessary before calling this a test
   of the explanation rather than a test of action transcription.
3. **Causal evidence:** Do claimed influences survive interventions or matched
   counterfactuals? A plausible strategic motive is not established by correct
   action prediction alone. Observation perturbations must be identified as
   perturbations, with their limits, rather than presented as recovered thoughts.

The decision-language comparison measures the first layer. A separately trained
observer tests whether the generated context contributes to action prediction;
matched pairs test whether descriptions distinguish different decisions on the
same map. Neither test alone establishes causal strategic reasoning.

## Correct action target

The local opponent uses `argmax` of the original raw logits, followed by action
decoding. Training rollouts sample from the logits. These are different targets.
All pass encodings should be merged when comparing probability distributions,
but merging them **before** taking an argmax changes the deployed greedy rule.

An audit of the older 3,072-example training set found 9.54% disagreement between
the merged-probability mode and the actual greedy rule. Even perfect knowledge
of the stochastic policy predicts a random sampled action only 55.29% of the
time on average. The reporting experiment therefore uses the actual deployment
rule and tracks sampled-policy quantities separately.

## Current experiments

The verified expanded dataset has 131,072 training snapshots from 6,119 distinct
maps, 8,192 validation snapshots from 364 maps, and 8,192 test snapshots from 378
maps. Map fingerprints do not overlap across splits. Instrumented activations
reproduce the original policy/value outputs exactly in these exports.

The spatial grounding comparison is documented in `SPATIAL_READER.md`. In
parallel, `modal_decision_training.py` compares two activation-to-language models:

- A spatial adapter feeding frozen Qwen3-0.6B.
- The same design with a learned action-probability auxiliary head and a
  continuous projection from that head into the language prefix.
- A separate mask-aware variant of that design, receiving the legal-move mask
  used by the original player as well as its hidden activations.

The auxiliary branch receives policy logits as training targets only. Both
branches receive detached hidden activations as input; neither receives the
chosen action, teacher logits, or observation labels at inference. The mask-aware
variant additionally sees legality, but never the selected move. All three use
the same data, shuffled batches, initial original adapter, four passes, and
4,096 updates. Each uses one B200. The independent observer uses one B200;
the final audit requests one only after these runs finish. Collection has
finished, and the campaign stays within the allowance of eight B200s.

### Frozen action-readout variant

The activation-only decoder's early validation results show frequent source and
direction errors despite fluent output. A fourth, distinct experiment
(`modal_readout_training.py`) copies the original frozen policy head into the
reader. Its input is still the detached hidden state plus legality. It reads the
preferred action internally, then adds six learned continuous language-prefix
embeddings for source row/column, direction, amount, source-patch context, and
preference strength. The words are generated by Qwen; no action sentence is
inserted by a template at inference.

This is decision-conditioned verbalization, rather than an independent probe of
why the player chose an action. The actor's action pathway remains unchanged.
This branch initializes from the completed spatial grounding reader and therefore
is a practical follow-up, not a matched ablation of the original three branches.
It uses one B200 for four passes. The copied head is a non-trainable buffer with
checkpoint provenance, and numerical agreement is audited separately from text
accuracy. Cross-framework low-precision arithmetic must not be assumed identical.
`modal_readout_audit.py` adds its held-out results to the other three variants.

The first B200 readout attempt stopped before training because its copied head
agreed with only 99.19% of the first 4,096 training decisions. A training-only
arithmetic diagnostic found that disabling reduced-precision BF16 reductions
and separately rounding the bias addition reproduced all 4,096 decisions.
Maximum logit error was still 0.03125; this is measured decision agreement, not
a claim of bit-identical cross-framework outputs. The corrected run separately
audits copied-head agreement and language accuracy on held-out data.

Supervision is generated automatically from the frozen player's greedy action
and visible observation. It describes the move and destination, with no claims
about hidden enemies or strategic motives. This is supervised distillation from
the player, not human annotation or training of the game policy.

## Audit and selection

The decision reader is evaluated on 512 fixed validation positions every 512
updates. A text-only parser reconstructs the stated move independently of the
activation and action targets. Selection prioritizes exact movement accuracy,
then complete descriptions with only supported template statements, then loss.
This parser is an explicit-action check, not an independently learned strategic
observer; it cannot by itself establish behavioral interpretation.

The selected checkpoint is evaluated on 1,024 held-out positions, including
shuffled and zero-activation controls. Results report parsing coverage, exact
action accuracy, movement-only accuracy, source/direction/split accuracy,
destination accuracy, and fully supported descriptions. Errors are counted
rather than replaced with correct teacher text. No-text or shuffled baselines
must not receive the player's hidden activations or outputs. Frozen weights are
hashed before and after training. Further model selection using these test
results requires a new untouched final audit set.

For the mask-aware reader, additional controls preserve the real legality mask
while zeroing or shuffling only the hidden activations. These distinguish
information from the player's representation from information supplied by the
rules of the game.

`modal_decision_listener.py` trains two matched observers from visible board
observations, temporal history, and legal moves: one with no explanation and one
with eight semantic context features. The latter features describe destination
ownership and movement relative to our general. The parser excludes the entire
explicit-action sentence, including source coordinates, direction, and troop
split. Training uses automatically derived context with dropout. Evaluation
compares generated, oracle, absent, and shuffled context on the same held-out
positions, with confidence intervals grouped by map. Neither observer receives
actor activations or logits as input.

`modal_decision_audit.py` waits for all reader and observer runs, then compares
the three selected readers on 128 pairs from different held-out maps. Within a
pair, the map and coarse board facts match but the player's greedy moves differ.
Pair selection does not depend on reader performance. These are observational
contrasts, not interventions. Reports also distinguish exact deployed tie-breaking
from any equally top-scoring action, and check legality and teacher logit regret.
The final reader is selected using validation scores only.

### Fresh audit and conditional iteration-3800 transfer

An additional 32,768 snapshots from 1,499 new maps are reserved for a final audit.
None of these maps occurs in the original train, validation, or test sets. Four
B200 workers evaluate the validation-selected reader, including rare half-troop
moves and independent context usefulness. This population is not used to choose
reader weights. The predeclared transfer-pilot criteria are at least 95% exact
move accuracy, 85% fully supported descriptions, 90% exact half-move accuracy
with at least 100 such examples, and a positive lower 95% map-bootstrap bound
for generated-context movement accuracy gain over the board-only observer.

`modal_reader_transfer.py` implements the user's conditional switch to iteration
3800. It waits for that audit and defers without allocating transfer GPUs if any
criterion fails. A pass supports a new-checkpoint pilot, not a claim of recovered
strategic reasoning. The source EMA checkpoint is read from the training volume
with a read-only mount. Iteration-2000 artifacts and deployed game players are
not replaced.

On a pass, four B200 workers collect 131,072 training snapshots plus 8,192 each
for validation and test from the frozen iteration-3800 EMA. Map fingerprints
must also be disjoint from all earlier iteration-2000 data. The selected 2000
adapter is initialized with the new player's frozen policy head, then adapted
for four passes (4,096 updates) on one B200. Initial validation measures transfer
before adaptation; validation alone selects the new reader. Original player and
language-backbone hashes are checked. Two independent observers are retrained
on the new policy's targets before auditing its generated context. The pilot's
1,024-position test must be reviewed before claiming reliable 3800 performance.

Tests validate action order, greedy-vs-sampling semantics, full/half encodings,
parser contradictions, rejection of extra unsupported sentences, unknown
destinations, frozen teacher gradients, spatial layout, and loss equivalence.

## Research basis

[Translate Policy to Language (ICLR 2026)](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.harvard.edu/dist/3/796/files/2026/02/12727_Translate_Policy_to_Lang-1.pdf)
uses third-party action prediction to evaluate explanations and keeps agent
decisions out of the explainer's prompt. We adopt the separation of explainer
inputs and decision targets, without assuming its reward model proves causality.
[Towards Faithful Model Explanation in NLP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11326)
provides the broader distinction between plausible language and faithful
explanations, including counterfactual and model-internal evidence.
